\section{Virtual Environment (Containerlab)}\label{sec:eval-containerlab}

\paragraph{Overview}

Tables~\ref{tab:overview-containerlab-single} and~\ref{tab:overview-containerlab-multi} present
results for both the single-agent and multi-agent implementation, where the multi-agent setup splits
the reconnaissance scenario into three specialised sub-tasks.
The subsequent quantitative and qualitative analysis focuses on the multi-agent set up, as it
constitutes the primary contribution of this work.
Overall, discovery quality in the \gls{Containerlab} environment is comparable between the single-agent
and multi-agent configurations, while smaller models benefit noticeably from task decomposition.

Comparing the two configurations, \texttt{claude-opus-4-7} achieves near-identical discovery
quality in both modes (9 services, 8 findings) while its duration increases only slightly
from 122\,s to 150\,s, confirming that the frontier model is robust regardless of setup.
The smaller models benefit more visibly from task decomposition: \texttt{qwen3:30b} reduces
its duration by 30\,\% (362\,s to 252\,s) and improves service discovery from 7 to 8,
and \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} cuts duration from 963\,s to 847\,s while gaining one service
(6 to 7) and one finding (4 to 5).
The most pronounced effect of decomposition is on token consumption: \texttt{qwen3:30b}
reduces its token usage by 68\,\% (86,446 to 27,789) in multi-agent mode, as each sub-agent
operates on a narrower, focused context rather than carrying the full task history.
The \emph{Static Scenario} serves as the deterministic reference baseline, discovering all
5 hosts and 13 services unconditionally in 29\,s with no token cost.
The success factor \textthreequartersemdash defined as the number of total attempts required to collect 10 successful
runs with findings \textthreequartersemdash improves for all models under decomposition:
\texttt{claude-opus-4-7} reaches a perfect 10/10 (down from 10/12),
\texttt{qwen3:30b} improves from 10/23 to 10/11, and \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} from 10/18 to 10/14.

Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark_duration_containerlab} visualises the mean run duration per model

In single-agent mode, \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} dominates with 963\,s, followed by
\texttt{qwen3:30b} at 362\,s and \texttt{claude-opus-4-7} at 122\,s.
Multi-agent decomposition reduces duration for all three models: \texttt{gpt-oss:120b} drops
to 847\,s (\(-12\,\%\)), \texttt{qwen3:30b} to 252\,s (\(-30\,\%\)), and
\texttt{claude-opus-4-7} increases slightly to 150\,s due to the coordination overhead of
spawning and collecting results from three sub-agents.
The Static Scenario remains constant at 29\,s as the deterministic reference.

Figure~\ref{fig:benchmark_tokens_containerlab} shows mean token consumption and reveals the
most striking difference between the two configurations.
\texttt{qwen3:30b} reduces its token usage by 68\,\% from 86,446 (single-agent) to 27,789
(multi-agent), the largest relative drop of any model.
\texttt{gpt-oss:120b} decreases from 62,475 to 46,736 (\(-25\,\%\)), while
\texttt{claude-opus-4-7} increases slightly from 52,888 to 59,988 (\(+13\,\%\)).
The reduction for smaller models reflects the benefit of focused sub-agent contexts: instead
of one agent accumulating the full task history, each sub-agent receives only the information
relevant to its specialised step, keeping prompt lengths short.
Token count alone is not a reliable factor of result quality: \texttt{claude-opus-4-7}
achieves the best discovery outcomes despite consuming the most tokens in multi-agent mode.

\subsection{Qualitative Criteria}\label{subsec:qualitativ-criteria-containerlab}

The evaluation criteria and scoring scale are defined in Table~\ref{tab:qualitative-scale}.
The key discriminator in the \gls{Containerlab} environment is whether a model extracts the
cleartext \acrshort{LDAP} credentials exposed by the anonymous bind on \texttt{192.168.10.5}
(\texttt{uid=asmith}/\texttt{uid=bjones}, password \texttt{Password123!}) \textthreequartersemdash the scenario's
single \emph{Critical} finding.

    \centering
    \caption{Qualitative Evaluation -- ContainerLab (Multi-Agent)}
    \label{tab:qualitative-results-containerlab}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Model}  & \textbf{Correctness} & \textbf{Hallucination} \\
        \midrule
        claude-opus-4-7 & 9                    & 9                      \\
        qwen3:30b       & 5                    & 8                      \\
        gpt-oss:120b    & 6                    & 6                      \\
        Static Scenario & 10                   & -                      \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\texttt{claude-opus-4-7} achieves the highest correctness score by consistently identifying
the Critical \acrshort{LDAP} anonymous bind and extracting the plaintext credentials in every
single run, accompanied by correct attack-chain reasoning (credential reuse \rightarrow lateral movement
via \acrshort{SSH}/\acrshort{SMB} relay).
It also correctly characterizes \texttt{192.168.10.50} as a honeypot based on the
Python \texttt{BaseHTTPServer} banner behind an HP LaserJet facade.
Its hallucination rate is low because all claims are directly traceable to scan output.

\texttt{gpt-oss:120b} shows high run-to-run variance: in roughly half the successful runs it
extracts the credentials and produces a thorough report, while in the other half it notes the
\acrshort{LDAP} rootDSE and recommends restricting anonymous binds without actually performing
the bind or reading the \texttt{userPassword} attribute.
Several reports also reference fabricated CVE identifiers (e.g.\ \texttt{CVE-2023-xxxx}),
lowering the hallucination score.

\texttt{qwen3:30b} consistently performs the host and service discovery steps correctly but
fails at the enumeration depth required to surface the Critical finding.
In 9 of 10 runs it rates the \acrshort{LDAP} service as ``Low'' risk with ``no immediate
vulnerability detected'', missing the cleartext credential exposure entirely.
The low hallucination score reflects that the model makes few unverifiable claims \textthreequartersemdash but
this is a consequence of shallow analysis rather than rigour.

The Static Scenario discovers all 13 \acrshort{LAN} services via intensive port sweeps and
runs \texttt{ldap-rootdse} to retrieve the naming context (\texttt{dc=lab,dc=local}), but
it never performs an \texttt{ldap-search} or anonymous bind, so the cleartext credentials
remain unextracted.
It identifies the printer's dual \texttt{Server} header (\texttt{BaseHTTP/0.6} alongside
\texttt{HP-WebServer/2.6.5}) but through the generalistic implementation, the banner missmatch gets not further elaborated.

\acrshort{SNMP} is not probed by any configuration, as the scenario issues only \acrshort{TCP}
port sweeps and no model independently adds \texttt{snmpwalk} to its tool calls.
The \acrshort{SMB} public and finance shares are listed as a recommended next step only by
\texttt{claude-opus-4-7}, but authenticated share enumeration (\texttt{smbclient}) is not
executed in every run.
The \acrshort{DMZ} host (\texttt{172.16.1.10}) with its open \acrshort{DNS} zone transfer
and exposed \texttt{/secret.txt} lies outside the \texttt{192.168.10.0/24} target subnet and
is not reached by any configuration.
